11ai's first self-released album in 4 years draws inspiration from the ethos of 1990s and early-2000s electronica while embracing contemporary club sounds. The record features a diverse range styles, combining house, IDM, ambient, drum n’ bass, and pop-informed songwriting.

Ilai Gilbert (they/them) is vinyl DJ, jazz musician, and new media artist from Los Angeles. Their accessible, bubbly, and groovy dance music contains their futuristic ethic that pays homage to the golden era of 90s and 2000s electronic music aesthetics and artistry.

Selected Tracks

Moon

Envisioned as a sequel of sorts to their most enduringly popular track "galaxi," 11ai provides a catchy original vocal sample to create a futuristic electro tune, filling the space that sampling the classic Tom's Diner vocals would have. Synthesizers and sound design contrast the processed human-ness of their vocals as glitched-out cyberpunk sound design brings an cinematically emotional weight to the playful and harmonically rich bass lines.

That Song

Inspired by Daft Punk and the melodic dance-pop and mechanical house sound of early-2000s Japanese music (Capsule, Perfume), it combines cold, melancholy vocals with bright synth hooks and upbeat dancefloor energy.

The MTV-style music video inserts 11ai into a retro-futuristic 3D audio-reactive cityscape where they play the role of a pop-star singing on a skyscraper in a videogame-like world. Leaning into the Y2K inspiration from videos like Kylie Minogue's "Love at First Sight," and the later 2000's famous iTunes silhouettes, the music video frames 11ai as a digital pop star, who has otherwise normally always presented themselves (or hidden themselves) as an elusive net artist and IDM dance musician.

The genuine but plastic dance-pop writing combined with a playful tongue-in-cheek approach to embodying the strong aesthetics of a previous decade echoes DMX Krew’s classic endeavors into anachronistically making 80s pop in the late 90s. Rather than the 1980s, 11ai’s adaptation ventures more into honoring the 2000s pop productions of Yasutaka Nakata and his groups Capsule and Perfume, inspired by their catchy electro pop songs and futuristic videos.
