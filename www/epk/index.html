Leapyear Register, 11ai's first self-released album in four years, pays homage to the adventurous spirit of 1990s and early-2000s electronica through eleven tracks inspired by house, IDM, ambient, drum & bass, and electro-pop. Each piece draws from different electronic traditions while remaining unified by 11ai's melodic songwriting, rich harmonic language, and distinctive production style.

Ilai Gilbert (they/them) is a musician and net artist from Los Angeles. With a background in jazz bass, electronic music, and creative coding, they have been DJing and releasing dance music under the alias 11ai since 2019. As a technologist, they create interactive media art and installations in the form of audiovisual touch screen synthesizers, interactive computer music installations, and web-based sound software.

Los Angeles-based musician and net artist 11ai creates bubbly and accessible dance music that balances emotional depth and rich harmonic complexity.

Singles / Music Videos

Moon / Flat

A spiritual successor to 11ai's most enduringly popular track galaxi, "Moon" features a catchy sample of the artist’s vocals that emulates the function of the classic Tom's Diner sample. Huge synths and glitched-out cyberpunk sound design contrast the processed human-ness of 11ai's vocals. Playful and harmonically rich bass lines dance over a futuristic Detroit and Miami-inspired electro groove.

Features a 3D music video, inspired by the graphics and colorway of the single's album cover: Watch on YouTube



That Song

Inspired by Daft Punk and the melodic and mechanical house sound of early-2000s J-pop, “That Song” combines cold, melancholy vocals with bright synth hooks and upbeat dancefloor energy.

The MTV-style music video inserts 11ai into a retro-futuristic 3D audio-reactive cityscape where they play the role of a pop-star singing on a skyscraper in a videogame-like world. Leaning into the 2000s inspiration from 3D pop music videos like Kylie Minogue's or Perfume’s, and the famous iTunes silhouettes, the video frames 11ai as a digital pop star, where normally they have historically presented (or hidden) themselves as an elusive net artist and IDM dance musician.

11ai's tongue-in-cheek yet ultimately genuine approach to channeling aesthetics of a previous decade echoes DMX Krew’s endeavors into anachronistically making 80s pop in the late 90s. 11ai’s adaptation honors the plastic-y 2000s pop productions of Yasutaka Nakata and his groups Capsule and Perfume, inspired by their catchy electro pop songs and futuristic videos.
